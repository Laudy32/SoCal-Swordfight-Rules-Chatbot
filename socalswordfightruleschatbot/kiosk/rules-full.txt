## Code of Conduct

Code of Conduct

SoCal Swordfight is dedicated to providing a safe, respectful, and inclusive environment for everyone. If any guests or staff violates this code of conduct, the event organizers may take any action they deem appropriate, including expulsion from the event without refund and a ban from all future SoCal Swordfight events.

We do not tolerate harassment or abuse of any kind. Harassment includes, but is not limited to, offensive language related to gender, sexual orientation, race, ethnicity, disability, religion (or lack thereof) or physical appearance, as well as deliberate intimidation, stalking, inappropriate physical contact, and unwelcome sexual attention.

Violations of safety include severely injuring another person due to an intentional action or neglecting to follow safety protocols given to you by staff, instructors, or vendors.

Report violations to one of our lead staff immediately. If you don’t know who they are, ask anyone wearing a SoCal Swordfight volunteer shirt.

## General Tournament Rules

General Tournament Rules

This document provides the rules common to all tournaments. This is meant to serve as a minimum standard. Each tournament will have additional rules covering quality standards, scoring actions, and illegal actions.

Rules are subject to change up to the commencement of the tournament.

Event Goals

At a high level, the goal of SoCal Swordfight tournaments is to showcase fencing which:

Displays good sportsmanship by everyone involved. If everyone isn’t having fun, then the event isn’t worth running.

Has a high level of competitive drive and promotes skill development and recognition.

Showcases historical techniques applied in a modern context.

Demonstrates a knowledge and familiarity with the use of sharp swords and high-quality striking mechanics.

Every year (we) the organizers hear “this is amazing, you have a huge event and yet everyone feels chill as if it is a much smaller community” and we want to keep it this way. We can all fight hard, strive for victory, and still come out better friends than when we started.

Top of Page

Forbidden Actions

Any strike thrown with unrestrained force on the part of the attacker. Competitive martial arts fighting involves throwing forceful strikes with full intent to make contact, however each competitor is expected to maintain a respect for their opponent’s safety at all times. Should a safety issue arise, a fighter must do everything in their power to arrest their strike in progress.

High amplitude throws and leg locks/wraps are prohibited in all tournaments. (Please refer to individual tournament rules to see if throws are permitted in any particular tournament.) A high amplitude throw is defined as any takedown action which either:

Raises the opponent’s hips above the level of their head in the execution.

Accelerates their opponent towards the ground, either through force and/or by lifting before releasing.

Directors WILL take into account the size difference between competitors when determining if sufficient control was demonstrated in a throw. If you are much larger than your opponent you are expected to be able to lower them down gently.

The ankle/foot is off target in all tournaments.

Exposing the spine or back of the head to the opponent. Note that if your opponent does expose such targets it is still your responsibility to do what you can to arrest the power of your strike.

Striking with a body part other than sword to deal percussive damage (punches, elbows, knees, headbutts, etc).
Want To Know More?↓

Shoving using the arms/body is expected in close quarters. At lower tiers judges will consider size disparities as a potential cause for an excessive force penalty.

Actions will only be considered 'punches' if they are a clear deliberate blow thrown as a punch. Aggressive hand movements in a grapple which contact the mask are to be expected.

Raising your leg between you and your opponent to keep or expand separation is not a kick, so long as you don’t have a strong impact or deliver a violent shove.

Dangerous Action - Actions that directors agree create a dangerous situation, such as diving for a target with no intention of landing on your feet, or throwing yourself on the ground for any reason.

Things that everyone knows are banned everywhere, such as attacks with the hilt or throwing anything.

Individual weapon tournaments may have additional rules in place. Be sure to read the rules for any tournaments you are entered in.

A dog may not participate in sword fighting matches.

Ring Outs

Should both of a fighter's feet leave the ring, a hold will be called and they will receive a non-safety penalty for a ring out (-1 pt). Should a fighter ring out with such speed that they leave the immediate ring area, they will receive a safety penalty.

Any action executed by a fencer who rings themselves out will "not have happened". 
Examples ↓

Example: [A] hits [B] and runs out of the ring. Result: [A] gets no points for their attack, and is penalized 1 point.

Example: [A] hits [B]. [B] delivers an afterblow as they ring themselves out. Result: [A] gets a clean hit, and [B] is penalized 1 point.

Example: [A] and [B] are wrestling at the edge of the ring, and [B] steps out of bounds. Result: [B] is penalized 1 point. (Don't wrestle at the edge of the ring to avoid this fate.)

This includes actions after the hold has been called. If you are running out of the ring after a hit has been delivered you will be penalized.

Top of Page

Match Conduct

All fighters will be expected to fight with both skill and control.

Fighters are expected to act professionally, and respectful to the officiating staff.

Fighters are expected to follow the commands of the tournament staff at all times.

This is a martial arts tournament and all fighters are expected to conduct themselves with respect for the weapon, their opponent, and the tournament staff. Any fighter that is deemed to pose a danger to themselves or others will be addressed at the discretion of the tournament director.

The tournament director will serve as the ultimate authority within the ring. The disciplinary procedure will generally constitute first a yellow card (warning), then a red card (penalty), then an expulsion from the match or tournament; however, it shall remain at the discretion of the director. Fighters who have been banned from attendance of previous SoCal Swordfight tournaments, or from other major HEMA tournaments, must contact staff before attempting to register for any tournaments.

Self-Calls

Fighters are able, and encouraged, to self-call attacks received; however, this is not required. A fighter who self-calls is only able to provide information to the match director, who may choose to use or discard the information as appropriate (eg, if the attack was flat). A fighter may only self-call in a manner which rewards their opponent. Valid self-calls are:

Indicating they were struck if judges missed the call.

Indicating they were struck in a higher value target than the judges are indicating.

Indicating that they did not strike the opponent or their strike lacked quality.

Indicating that they struck the opponent in a lower value target than indicated.

If a fighter being awarded a clean hit self-calls a Double Hit the exchange will be recorded as a No Exchange.

If a fighter in a Double Hit calls their strike back it is up to the director if they will accept the self call or not, depending on their certainty of the original ruling.

Penalties

Penalties will be assessed as Safety or non-Safety. Safety penalties are escalating in nature, and follow the following escalation:

Yellow Card - Warning

Red Card - Point deduction (3 points)

Black Card - Match forfeit

Expulsion from the tournament

Based on the severity of the infraction a director may choose to skip steps of escalation. Safety penalties are tracked across all tournaments and the director will base the escalation of the first offense based on the fighter’s prior safety record.

Non-Safety penalties (aka White Cards) are a 1 point deduction, and do not result in any escalation or disciplinary action.

Any fighter who is awarded a penalty will have any scoring actions they performed be invalidated (aka they never happened).

Promptness

All fighters are expected to be ready to fight as soon as their match is called. This includes:

Being queued up behind their corner.

Having all their gear on.

Not being ready when your match is called will result in a penalty. It may only be a few seconds to you, but it adds up over hundreds of matches! Be courteous to everyone's time and be ready to start with no delays.

Match Length

Point Cap

All matches will be conducted until a point cap of 7 points is reached.

Time Limit

Matches will run using a stopped clock. At no point will a hold be called based on the clock expiring, and all time-outs will be based on the current time after the director has called for an exchange to end.

If no fighter reaches the point cap before 90 seconds the match will enter overtime. If a fencer in the lead is awarded a point after overtime begins they will win the match. However if they are unable to gain a point then the match will continue. (Aka if you are behind after 90 seconds you still have the potential for a comeback over multiple exchanges. If you are winning after 90 seconds you need to only score a single point to seal the deal.)

Should the clock exceed 150 seconds (2.5 min) at the conclusion of an exchange the match will hit the hard-cap and the fighter with the higher score will be declared the winner.

Examples ↓

Example: The score is 1-5 and [A] scores 3 points at 95 seconds into the match. The score is now 4-5 and the match continues until either: [A] is able to get ahead of [B], [B] is able to score a point, or the hard-cap is reached and [B] wins.

Example: The score is 4-4 and [B] scores 1 point on an exchange that ends at 92 seconds into the match. [B] has earned a go-ahead point after the soft-cap and wins the match 4-5.

Example: The score is 4-1 and the fighters double at 91 seconds into the match. The match continues. A second double is recorded at 103 seconds, and the match continues. [A] is able to score 2 points at 127 seconds and wins the match 6-2.

Bracket Advancement

After the conclusion of the pools the fighters will be ranked by indicator score. The top 50% will be advanced into a single elimination bracket.
(You can click on "How are the standings calculated?" at the bottom of the Pool Standings to see the ranking algorithm for any tournament.)

All finals except Tier-A gold medal matches will be fought at the conclusion of their tournament bracket. For Tier-A tournaments the bronze medal match will be fought at the conclusion of the tournament bracket, and the gold medal match will be fought during the Grand Finals on Sunday Afternoon. Matches in the Grand Finals will be a best-of-three format, and all other medals will be determined by a single match. (For the purpose of the Grand Finals the URG Saber and Rapier tournaments are Tier-A and will be in the Grand Finals, while the U18 tournaments are not Tier-A and will not be in the Grand Finals.)

Scoring Actions

Quality Standards

Points will be awarded for cuts/thrusts/slices/etc. in accordance with the rules of the individual tournaments. Scoring actions must display:

Deliberate intent and targeting on the part of the attacker.

Sufficient depth. Cuts with the tip of the sword or shallow thrusts are not scoring actions.

Cuts

Cuts must be delivered with sufficient cutting plane, body structure, and blade arc.

Cutting plane is defined as the blade moving in line with a deliberate plane (edge alignment) and the sword moving along such a plane for the position of its movement immediately before striking the target. Special attention will be paid to horizontal strikes, as these typically display poor cutting plane and edge alignment.

Example ↓

Example ↓

You laugh at this example, but look at some slow-motion footage of your horizontal cuts. :p 

Sufficient body structure, which is not indicated by how hard the cut hits, but in demonstrating connection between the body and sword at the time of impact. 
Example ↓

Textbook good cut. Arms are extended, the blade/crossguard are in line with the plane of motion on impact. 

A sword must display rotation before contact with the target, typically in excess of 60 degrees. Arc should not be considered the only determinant of quality, as some strikes may be acceptable given a very good cutting plane and body structure with shorter arc, and cuts with no structure/cutting plane will not be valid no matter how large the arc.

Thrusts

Thrusts must demonstrate the ability to penetrate into the target beyond a superficial depth. This is at a bare minimum of at least 3” into the target and can be demonstrated by a bend of the blade or visible force reaction at the point of contact. Should a fighter deliberately shorten the extension of their arm to lower the force of a thrust it will also be considered as a quality attack.

Example ↓

Example ↓

Example ↓

If you are stepping in deeply with your thrusts, it is expected that you relax your arms on impact. 

Example ↓

Example ↓

Example ↓

It is understood that due to the design of HEMA masks a thrust to the mesh will have a high instance of being deflected on contact. It is further understood that the movement of a fighter may allow them to ‘roll out’ of contact from a thrust. A thrust that ‘glances off’ is a product of our protective gear, real sword points stick! (Though a thrust that misses and is pushed against the target with the flat is of course not valid, no matter how much the blade bends.)

Slices

Slices will only be considered as valid when performed against the upper opening and the arm (slices to the off-arm in 1h weapons do not count). The slice must show purposeful placement of the edge with sufficient force to display a clear reaction from the target. This may be demonstrated by pushing the target back at the point of contact, or simply preventing the target from being able to move freely. Drawing motion is not required, however note that this push requires a high degree of commitment, and is not simply sawing a blade back and forth on the body.

Example ↓

Example ↓

Either de-stabilizing your opponent, or interfering with their movement in some way. (Doesn't have to stop them in their tracks, but we should be able to see a reaction in their body.) 

Example ↓

Pommel Strikes

Pommel strikes may be indicated to the mask from any direction, but making contact with any surface other than the front of the mask is illegal. Pommels must demonstrate deliberate and unimpeded action, as well as (if contacting the mesh) control of force.

Pommel strikes do not need to make contact to be considered valid, provided there is an unambiguous path from the termination of the strike and the target. Example ↓

Example ↓

Pommel strikes to the top or side of the mask are not scoring actions, and will incur a penalty if delivered deliberately and/or with force. 

Pommel strikes which do make an impact must demonstrate control of force and respect for your opponent’s safety. Example ↓

The motion of the sword must be in line with the pommel. Wildly pushing your sword hilt back and forth in a high bind does not constitute a pommel strike. Example ↓

While it wouldn't be pleasant to be on the receiving end, we want fighters to display a controlled and deliberate action. These are not valid scoring actions. (Doing the same thing, but harder, is also not appreciated.) 

Targets

Target Area Definitions

“Upper opening”, for the purpose of these rules, includes both the head and cuts landing on the top of the torso, in an area from shoulder seam to shoulder seam. This is to both reduce judging error for head strikes, and reward fencing based on intention (fencing for high targets) rather than luck (they moved their head out of the way). Should there be any lack of clarity if a blow struck the “upper opening” or arm target area a default of arm shall be taken - because you already missed the head by a wide margin.

Arm strikes will also include hand strikes. A strike to the handle of the sword will be considered as a strike to the hands. Note that full quality requirements are also in place for strikes to the hands.

Torso is the remaining area above the waist, Leg is below the waist. (Typically indicated by the bottom of the jacket.)

The foot/ankle is an illegal target in all tournaments

In the event of disagreement from the judges on the target area struck the point value will be decided at the discretion of the director. Typically, this will mean defaulting to the lower value target.

Blade Grabs & Disarms

Disarms, intentional or accidental, are not scoring actions. A fighter must follow up a disarm with a valid scoring action (they don’t have a sword, just give them a light tap!). Should a fighter lose their weapon they may also concede a maximum point strike instead of attempting to enter distance for a grapple. (Running away is considered conceding.)

A blade may only be grabbed if it is not in motion, otherwise it will be counted as a scoring action to the hand. If the sword was in the process of attacking a high point value target, Target Substitution rules will apply.

Target Substitution

If an arm is struck and

It is not currently holding the sword,

The strike was likely to be a high value strike had it not hit the arm (cut/thrust head, thrust torso),

the attack will instead be registered against the higher value target. This is a point substitution, and not a warning/cardable offense.

Timing Of Actions

Double Hits

A double hit is defined as any time both fighters land valid scoring actions that were “in-flight” at the same time. It is possible for the hits to not be simultaneous, so long as the later hit was already moving forward when the first hit landed.

Example: [A] is circling their sword around to chamber when the strike from [B] lands, after which [A] swings the sword forward and strikes [B]. The attack from [A] was not started before they were struck, and it is not a double hit. (See the section on afterblows.)

Example: [A] see’s [B] circle their sword back to chamber, and launches an attack into the opening. By the time [A]’s strike lands the attack from [B] has already begun moving forward, and the attack from [B] lands shortly after. This is a double hit because both attacks were in flight at the same time.

Example ↓

They both hit each other at the same time. Double. 

Example ↓

Even though the attacks do not hit at the same time, they are in motion at the same time. 

Example ↓

This is stretching the definition of a double hit to the limit. There was no defensive reaction by the slower fighter, and both attacks were in flight at the same time. 

Double Hits are recorded as no match score for each fighter, and are factored into the overall indicator score. There is an increasing deduction for each double hit, cumulative over the entire pool. (-1/-2/-3/ etc).

If 3 double hits are recorded in a pool match, the match will end with no winner.

If a double hit is very obviously the result of one party ignoring the threat of the opponent’s weapon an additional penalty will be assessed to this fighter.

Example: Attacks to the lower torso or legs when the opponent is chambered to cut to the upper opening.

Afterblow Definition

The afterblow is a valid scoring hit landing as the next action after a clean (aka non-double) hit. Any attack, step, or feint consumes the chance for an afterblow. An afterblow will deduct -1 from the point value of the initial attack’s score.

Example ↓

Example ↓

This is and afterblow because the initial clearing defensive action failed, and the cut to the head was a follow up. 

Example ↓

Example ↓

Pulling of Attacks

In just about all cases an attack that is “pulled” before it makes impact is not a valid scoring action. To be awarded points you must strike your opponent! (Though it is certainly considered good sportsmanship to strike as softly as possible when presented with an opening your opponent has no hope of defending.)

The exception to this rule is if their opponent has created an unsafe situation through their own actions (eg: expose back of the head, fall down). Should there be any risk to safety, for any reason, a fighter is expected to make every effort to pull back a strike in progress and/or not launch a strike to begin with. However, if a fighter can demonstrate they retained the wherewithal and capacity to launch an attack they will receive the maximum allowed point value for the exchange.

Note: Pommels strikes are an exception to this rule, and may be presented IF it is unambiguous that there is no obstacle to the target. (ie no hand in the way).

Example: A fighter turns the back of the head during an exchange and their opponent stops their blow in mid-flight. The fighter who was able to arrest their attack will be awarded points, and the fighter who exposed the target will be penalized.

Should a dangerous situation arise due to an action outside of the fighter's control (eg: gear failure) both fighters should immediately stop fighting and any actions delivered during or after the safety hold will not be valid scoring actions.

Example: A fighter’s elbow pad falls off and they hit their opponent in the next action. It will be assumed that their opponent was attempting to withdraw at the sight of the equipment failure, and therefore they were not fully committed to the fencing actions occurring.

## Longsword Rules

Longsword Rules

This document provides the rules specific to the Longsword Tournaments. All rules outlined in the General Tournament Standards also apply to the longsword tournaments, and offer more depth on the criteria for scoring actions.

Rules are subject to change up to the commencement of the tournament.

Changes

Everyone kept misunderstanding what “control” was. We now have “bound” strikes with a very specific criteria. (Blades touching when hitting, or grappling)

Shin protection now required for LS.

U18 is now completely steel BYOS. 

Tournament Goal

The goal of the Longsword tournament is to both showcase and help develop skill in utilizing the longsword for the purpose of unarmored combat, as outlined by the KdF tradition. (Of course, all other traditions are invited to play!) This includes the use of the longsword as a sharp sword. Tournament rules and judging standards are meant to advance fundamental skills, such as integrating cutting mechanics into antagonistic sparring. A match is not a simulation of a sword duel.

In the use of a longsword there are times when you would wish to use different types of striking mechanics, but if you don’t demonstrate high quality strikes when asked to, it shows you are not capable of them. It is the goal of SoCal Swordfight to stress test high quality striking interpretations and push the fighters to achieve these. There are many other existing venues which test a fencer's skill and ability to achieve touches as quickly as possible.

SoCal Swordfight will seed fighters into appropriate tournament tier based on results of past longsword tournaments, with the aim of creating as many meaningful matches as possible. (aka matches where you are close in skill to your opponent.)

Cutting Qualification

All participants competing in Open Longsword must complete a cutting qualification.

If you completed the cutting qualification at SoCal 2024, SoCal 2025, or have competed (with some degree of success) in a cutting tournament at any event in 2024 or 2025, you are already qualified. If not, you must attend a cutting qualification on Friday or you will be disqualified from the longsword competition. (There is no limit on the number of re-tries to pass the qualification). 

Even if you think you are pre-qualified you are responsible for checking the cutting qualification list to be sure. You can check by going into your longsword tier and selecting Cutting Qualification, next to the Tournament Roster.

Cutting qualification shall consist of the following 4 cuts, given 6 total attempts. (in any order):

1 clean descending cut through the paper from the right.

1 clean descending cut through the paper from the left.

1 descending cut from the right that is arrested past the half way mark of the paper, but before the end (half-cut).

1 descending cut from the left that is arrested past the half way mark of the paper, but before the end (half-cut). 

The paper is quite wide, approximately 2 feet, so you will need to maintain your sword in the cutting plane for a relatively wide arc. While the paper will not stop your blade like a tatami mat, if you cut poorly it will rip apart rather than cutting.

Illegal Actions

All illegal actions from the General Tournament Rules apply to this tournament, including but not limited to: lack of control/excessive force, high amplitude throws, or anything else that has a high chance to hurt somebody. Refer to the General Tournament Rules for more details.

Exposing the spine or back of the head to the opponent. Note that if your opponent does expose such targets it is still your responsibility to do what you can to arrest the power of your strike.

Should a fighter execute one handed blows outside of the grapple they may be awarded an unsporting conduct penalty (-1 pt) at the discretion of the director.

Throws/takedowns are only permitted in Longsword Open Tier-A & Tier-B, and URG Tier-A. No throws/takedowns in Open Tier-C & Unrated, URG Tier-B, and U18.

Scoring Actions

Matches will be fought until a fighter reaches a score of 7 points.

3 Point Actions:

Cut/Thrust to Upper Opening

Thrust to Torso

Standing Takedown*
*Only in Open Tier-A, Open Tier-B, and URG Tier-A

1 Point Actions:

All other Cuts & Thrusts

Slices to Upper Opening or Arm

Pommel Strike to Mask

+1 point for Bound Action

Afterblow and Double Hits will be scored in accordance with the General Tournament Rules. (Afterblow is -1 from initial score. Don’t double.)

One-handed strikes are not permitted except when the hand is removed as part of a grappling technique executed against an opponent.

Bound Action

A Bound Action will award an extra (1) point to the scoring action. If either of the following are true it is a Bound Action:

The sword is in contact with the opponent’s blade when the scoring action lands.

The opponent’s sword has been controlled by a grapple before the scoring action lands.

Bound Actions are not about "control"! If the swords are in contact when the hit lands it is a bound strike. You can in theory afterblow a Bound Action if you remove the sword and hit in the same action.

Wrestling and Takedowns
Wrestling

The amount of time permitted for a wrestling action is a 5-count from the director; beginning when both of the swords are inhibited (discarded, entangled, etc). This count may or may not be verbalized at the director's discretion.
Throws/Takedowns

Throws/takedowns are only permitted in Longsword Open Tier-A & Tier-B, and URG Tier-A. No throws/takedowns in Open Tier-C & Unrated, URG Tier-B, and U18. As per the General Tournament Rules high amplitude throws and leg wraps/locks are not permitted.

A standing takedown is a wrestling action that deliberately and safely puts the opponent on the ground, while the throwing fighter remains standing. Standing requires one foot remain on the ground, dropping to a single knee is acceptable.

For a takedown to qualify as a Bound Action, the throwing fighter must retain control of their weapon and demonstrate the ability to deliver an attack.

Equipment

All protective equipment and weapons must be inspected prior to competition.

Protective Equipment

All equipment from the General Tournament Standards is required, in addition to the following:

Purpose-built Heavy HEMA Gloves (eg: Thokk or better). Lacrosse / Red Dragon style gloves, regardless of modification, are explicitly not allowed.

Heavy padded HEMA jacket.

Shin protection is required.

Forearm protection is not required but recommended.

Weapons

A 'standard' straight bladed longsword feder is expected ("shiltless feders" designed for high intensity sparring are also permitted. Thin edged 'blunts' are not.).

The point must have an expanded surface area, or be affixed with a (non-rubber) tip of some sort.

Blades must be free of any large burs or hilt protrusions which may cause a risk to an opponent's safety or their equipment.

Sword measurements are expected to fall within these ranges:

Blade Length: 84 - 102 cm (33 - 40")

Overall Length: < 140 cm (55”)

Sword Mass: 1.3 - 1.8 kg (2.9 - 3.9 lbs. )

Buckling Strength: 10 - 19.5 kg (How is this measured?)

Swords must be from a HEMA manufacturer.

Final discretion lies with the tournament manager, including judgment on blade design elements that may not be included in this document. The event is under no obligation to allow you to use the sword you have brought.

## Rapier Rules

Rapier Rules

This document provides the rules specific to the rapier tournaments. All rules outlined in the General Tournament Standards also apply to the rapier tournaments, and offer more depth on the criteria for scoring actions.

Rules are subject to change up to the commencement of the tournament.

Tournament Goal

The goal of this rule set is to promote a style of fencing that is skillful, artful and martial, as well as representative of the 17th century rapier treatises that the vast majority of the HEMA community currently studies. We wish to encourage fencing which showcases the techniques described in the treatises for dueling with sharp rapiers, while acknowledging that a tournament match can never be a recreation of an actual duel.

Participants are only able to register for Single Rapier, or Rapier & Dagger. SoCal Swordfight will then seed fighters into appropriate tournament tier based on results of past tournaments, with the aim of creating as many meaningful matches as possible. (That means that there will be Single Tier-A, Single Tier-B, Rapier+Dagger Tier-A, and Rapier+Dagger Tier-B.)

Illegal Actions

All illegal actions from the General Tournament Rules apply to this tournament, including but not limited to: lack of control/excessive force, high amplitude throws, or anything else that has a high chance to hurt somebody. Refer to the General Tournament Rules for more details.

Exposing the spine or back of the head to the opponent. Note that if your opponent does expose such targets it is still your responsibility to do what you can to arrest the power of your strike.

All “Body Wrestling” is forbidden. This includes throws, joint locks, and pushing a fighter out of bounds. (It is, of course, still legal to pressure an opponent to back up even if they are at the edge of the ring.)

Blade grabs of stationary blades are permitted; however the blade must be released after a single action. Should a fighter continue to hold the blade they will be assessed a non-safety penalty of 1 point.

Strikes with the pommel or hilt are not permitted.

This includes the use of the saber as a sharp sword. Tournament rules and judging standards are meant to advance fundamental skills, such as integrating cutting mechanics into antagonistic sparring. Quality Standards

In each round of the cutting tournaments each cut (single cut or multiple cut task as explained in each round) is worth a total of 10 possible points. Scoring and quality standards are the same for Longsword and Arming Sword cutting.

Each judge will be tasked with scoring a certain portion of the cut (person or mat) and take note of deductions observed. Both the cutting judge and form judge will record the highest deduction by either, and they will be combined for the final score for the cut. (In the case of a full point deduction in either Cut or Form the final points will be zero, and not consider the other deduction.)

Final Score = 10 - √(CutDeduction2 + Form Deduction2)

(If you’re not a math person you can look at the table at the end of this section for examples.)

'x' indicates that no points will be awarded. [-1/-2/-3] indicates the deduction is -1 point in Tier-C, -2 points in Tier-B, -3 points in Tier-A.

Cut Quality

'x' indicates that no points will be awarded.

[-5]: Small Mountain Example↓

A mountain is when a cut intersects a previous cut or cut attempt. This is penalized because the competitor no longer needs to cut through the full width of the target, and also because it demonstrates poor targeting and control. Small mountains will be assessed a point deduction of -5 points.

[-3/-5/-8]: Tatami Landing Distance (yellow) Example↓

[X]: Tatami Landing Distance (red) Example↓ [Why So Harsh?]

When the piece flies to the side it often indicates sub-par edge alignment that was facilitated by good structure and/or strength. While technique is the most important part of cutting, it is also true that physical strength does confer an advantage. By discounting tatami pieces which fly off to the side we are seeking to reduce the contribution that strength has on scoring points, and reward competitors for cleaner technique.

'Landing Zones' will be used to assess cutting quality. These zones will be clearly marked on the ground around each cutting stand.

The zone deduction is assessed based on the position at which the tatami first comes into contact with the ground (i.e. rolling after contact does not impact the score). 

In the event that a clearly defined piece cannot be identified, the cut will be assumed to have landed in the red zone. 

Should a cut spray debris, it will be assumed to have landed in the red zone.

Should a piece land straddling two zones, it will be assessed as landing in the lower deduction zone.

[X] Scallop Example↓

A scallop is defined as a cut which clearly curves its trajectory in the tatami mat. This also applies to any parallel mat cuts which do not have the two cuts in the same line.

[X] Large Mountain Example↓

A mountain is when a cut intersects a previous cut or cut attempt. Competitor Conduct

Competitors shall not begin cutting until instructed to do so by the tournament staff. Following the completion of each cut, the competitor must wait for confirmation from the cutting judges before beginning their next cut. After assessing the quality of a cut, the cutting judges will hold up a color-coded card to provide feedback to the cutter and the audience and to indicate that the competitor may make their next cut. A competitor may not address the table to inquire about a score which has been awarded. These cards will be: 

Green - No point deduction on cut

Yellow - Point deduction on cut

Red - No points awarded on cut

Verbal Warning - Penalty assessed

Should a competitor request a mat straightening (provided the round allows for it), they must first step out of the cutting area with the tip of their sword pointed toward the ground. The competitor must not move until instructed to do so by the tournament staff.

Competitors that take excessive time to begin after they have been signaled to cut will be warned by the tournament staff, and may be subject to penalties if there are repeated infractions. While it is not necessary for a competitor to rush after they are prompted, it is intended that they perform the cut in a short period of time without spending considerable time fidgeting or psyching themselves up.

During reactive cutting rounds, the competitor must wait in an area designated by the tournament staff. Only once the cut has been signaled may they move forward and execute their cut. The competitor must then return to the waiting area before the signaling period has concluded or be assessed a point deduction on the cut.

Judging A Match

Procedure

Movement
After the director instructs the fighters to begin your role is to observe to the best of your ability. Unfortunately the fighters are focused on their opponent and not making sure they stay in a good line of sight.

Keep Moving - If the fighters are moving around the ring, you should be moving. In addition to helping you get a good angle, movement helps you stay focused and awake while watching the fight.

Get an Angle - Judging is often the “best seat in the house” so use it to your advantage. The ideal angle is between 30-60 degrees to either side of the fighters, not perpendicular to them. Judges are allowed inside the ring as well, so get closer when they move to the other side of the ring.

Spread Out - Be aware of where the director and judges are in your periphery. Avoid having three Judges on the same side of the fighters. Try to stay at least a quarter of the ring away from the other Judges.

Change Direction - Some fighters circle around each other in one direction. You might have to run to and keep up or get dizzy trying, so feel free to change directions. Watch other judges for cues that they are switching as well.

Notes On Scoring Actions

You should be familiar with the General Tournament Rules, and the rules of the tournament you are judging. The following are some quick points to keep in mind with regards to making the calls on different actions, and not a complete definition.

Types of Actions

Indicate the target and the type of attack (cut/thrust/slice). If you're not sure on the target indicate the lower value option.

Double Hits are in flight at the same time. They are NOT necessarily attacks that hit at the exact same moment.

An afterblow is launched AFTER someone has been hit.

Doubles and afterblows are defined by action and not time. There is no "out of tempo", it is either the first thing they do after being hit (afterblow) or it isn't (late).

In the longsword tournaments a bound action is defined as a blow which is in contact with the opponent’s blade when the hit happens or primary weapon arm/sword is controlled by a grapple at the moment of impact. 

Scoring Actions

None of the quality standards should be confused for a requirement to hit hard. You should indicate why you are calling no quality so the fighter knows how to improve their strikes on the next pass.

Cuts should demonstrate (1) a solid cutting plane with the edge aligned, (2) body structure that demonstrates connection between the body and sword up until the point of impact (but not necessarily during), (3) rotational energy, and (4) hit with more than just the tip of the sword.

Thrusts should demonstrate more than a superficial contact, either through flexing, collapsing of the thrusters arms on contact, or continued forward motion after being deflected off a rigid surface like a mask. Keep your eyes open, just because it didn't stick doesn't mean it didn't hit!

Slices DO NOT require blade travel. They just must display a strong force reaction stopping/displacing the head or arms.

Pommel Strikes are only valid to the front of the mask. They do not need to connect if the path is unopposed.

Ring Outs are scored by the director, but you should be careful not to award strikes delivered by a fighter who was in the process of leaving the ring.

Takedowns require one fighter to remain on at least one knee, or standing. No points if both fighters go down.

Safety & Penalties

As the director you are always responsible for the ring, and the safety of anyone in the vicinity. Take all possible steps to ensure no risky situations develop. Not all possibilities are explicitly laid out in these guidelines and your experience is valued and necessary in anticipating potential issues. In you have and questions or concerns please confer with the Tournament Manager.

Safety

You are under no obligation or responsibility to provide first-aid. Your job is only to make sure that the event first-aid is notified and the area is secured.

Ensure that all fighters are adhering to the equipment standards at all times, including calling hold in the event of a mid-match equipment issue.

Ensure that all event safety policies are followed in the ring.

Ensure that no one other than the fighters and judging staff enter the ring while matches are ongoing.

Above all else, bring your experience and judgement to bear in anticipating issues. This safety section isn't short because it is unimportant, it is short because it can't replace the expertise of a skilled director.

Penalties

Verbal Warnings

Don't give verbal warnings. A warning is a yellow card.

If you feel a fighter may escalate to unsafe behavior in the future you are encouraged to remind the fighter(s) of the tournament rules and standards. If they did something which is in violation of the rules they MUST have a card issued.

Example: There is an intense match and every exchange the levels of force increase slightly. The director informs both fighters “you can keep fighting at this intensity, but if you go any higher you will be penalized”.

Example: A fighter turns the back of their head towards their opponent in an exchange. If the director gives a verbal warning and fails to issue a card it means they have deemed this blatant violation of the safety policy acceptable.

Penalties can be either safety or non-safety. Announce the penalty card, point deduction, and infraction to the table so that they are able to record it.
A fighter can not receive points on an exchange where they receive a penalty.

Non-Safety Penalties

Non-safety penalties are point deductions, but do not have any escalation and competitors can do them as much as they want (if they for some reason want lose points every time. ¯\_(ツ)_/¯ ). They are all worth -1 point. Excessive abuse of a non-safety penalty may constitute Failure To Follow Instructions.

(For the purpose of the table staff a non-safety penalty is a white card.)

Safety (aka escalating) Penalties

Safety penalties are tracked over all matches and escalate; before each match check with the table staff to see if the fighters have any previous safety penalties. If a penalty is severe, the director may skip to a higher color immediately.

If someone is seemingly playing bingo and doing a different safety penalty in each of their matches you should probably be concerned and escalate at your discretion. The guidelines represent minimums.

Excessive Force/Lack of Control - 
Example ↓ 

If a fighter lacks control to the point of endangering themselves or their opponent, issue this penalty. This includes not making an effort to pull a strike during a dangerous situation (eg: opponent exposes the back of their head or falls down), as well as excessively hitting the ground due to losing control of the weapon or being off balance. Or any situation that makes you think "this could get someone hurt".

Off-Target Attack - 
Example ↓ 

The ankle/foot is off target in all tournaments due to safety of the fighters and attacks which are aimed to this target are penalties. (This does not include attacks which are aimed at valid targets, but end up missing and following through.) 

Failure To Follow Instructions - 
Example ↓ 

The fighter should adhere to all conduct rules and instructions of the director. If the fighter displays disrespect for the director's authority to manage the ring it is a safety issue by default. 

Continuing After Hold - 
Example ↓ 

If a fighter continues excessively after a hold is called, issue this penalty.

Exposing Back - 
Example ↓ 

Fighters must not expose their back, including the back of their head, while they are engaged with their opponent, including immediately after the hold call. (If one fighter exposes their head while the other fighter does not attempt to pull the strike, assign this penalty to the first fighter and “Excessive Force” to the other fighter.)

ANY other infraction - 
Example ↓ 

There are many possible safety infractions resulting from the reckless or negligent behavior of a fighter. If something is a problem it does not have to fall exactly into one of these categories. Check with the tournament manager if you are unsure. (Never let an potentially risky scenario develop just because it wasn't explicitly addressed in the rules.)

Running A Match

Communication

Communication to fighters should always be as clear as possible. If the call required some synthesis of information be sure to explain to the fighters clearly how it was determined. (In as quick and concise a way as possible.)

Should a strike not be awarded points you MUST explain why. Words like "No Quality" or "Tippy" should never be used as they are not explicitly clear what the fighter must do in the future, and may result in them getting frustrated and hitting harder. Instead use terms like "not enough rotation" or "contact is too close to the tip"

Though a fighter or coach may never question a call they may respectfully ask to address the director, and then ask for clarification if they are unclear.

Judge Agreement

The majority of final calls should be determined by the judges' signals only. This is to keep directing as consistent as possible over all matches and pools. The director must provide a brief explanation to those around the ring in order to justify the override.

If at least 2 judges are in full agreement, an override should be almost NEVER. In extremely rare situations, a conference can be called and an override may take place if the judges change their signals.

If no judges are in full agreement, these situations can result in a conference and/or override.

Target Discrepancy: If 2 judges agree that there was a scoring action for the same fighter but disagree on target, the director should almost always split the scoring difference. (ex.: Gold Leg + Gold Torso = Split) Note: Some scores are the same, so don’t spend too much time on this.

Possible Double: If 1 judge calls a double and the other 2 judges call for opposite fighters, the director may override rarely. (ex.: Double + Gold Arm + Black Arm)

In the end, everything is technically up to the director’s discretion, but the optics of fairness and remaining unbiased should be part of the final call.

Conferences

Conferences should be avoided if at all possible. If you need to ask a question just yell across the ring.

If a conference is necessary the director should quickly as a neutral and non-biasing "What did you see?" to each judge. Each judge should respond with a short and to the point answer and not a story. The conference is for the director to gather information, not to have a conversation. Once they have heard the judges answers they make the final call.

Private conferences should always be followed by the judges re-signaling their calls, and a brief public explanation of the final call.

If something isn’t clear, the director should wash the exchange instead of trying to get every call “correct”.
